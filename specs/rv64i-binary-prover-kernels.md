## Summary

An experiment in RV64I hash-based Jolt over binary fields proves each cycle from 256 committed bits and a per-cycle R1CS witness of 1,024 bits, with every sum-check over `F128` (`specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md`). Its reference tier holds one dense table of field elements per leaf of a relation, 16 bytes for each of those bits, and multiplies in the field where a word operation suffices; it is a test oracle, usable to about `2^10` cycles. This spec adds `jolt-rv64i-kernels` with three things. The first is the machinery every production kernel of the experiment shares for tables of bits: lifts through lookup tables, bucket accumulation in scratch that is bounded per worker, scatters partitioned by destination, round messages built in coefficient form, and the rule for when bits become field elements. The second is a benchmark harness: seeded synthetic profiles, one runner that every kernel benchmark goes through, and a probe that measures the unit costs the performance model assumes. The third is the cores of the members that take the most sum-check time by operation count, the Spartan outer sum-check over the rows with values in `F_2` and the routers, and the cores of the tail of the protocol: the two chunk products and the reduction of the committed claims of batch 6b, with the pass that builds the reduction's tables and the pass that produces the 256 column values. Each core stands alone, is tested against the definition of its sum, and is benchmarked through the runner at `2^20` and `2^22` cycles before any relation type or stage driver exists.

## Intent

### Goal

Build the packed-bit machinery, the benchmark harness and the cores of the bitwise outer sum-check, the routers, the chunk products and the reduction of committed claims with its two passes over the committed table, each emitting exactly the round polynomials of its definition within a stated number of nanoseconds per cycle.

**Notation.** `T = 2^log_t` cycles, indexed by `j`. A point lists its low variable first and every kernel binds low to high (`BindingOrder::LowToHigh`): coordinate `i` of a point belongs to variable `i`, which is bit `i` of the index the point is paired with, as in `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md` §3. This is the order of every point a core, a pass or `oracle` takes or returns, and of the challenge list `prove_batch` returns, which is used as it is. The equality helpers of `jolt-poly` take the high variable first, and `round::eq` is the one place that converts (Design, "Equality over cycles"). `eq(w, x) = Π_i (1 + w_i + x_i)`. A sum-check is defined by a summand `g` built from multilinear extensions of tables over a Boolean cube; its round-`i` polynomial is `s_i(t) = Σ_x g(r_1, …, r_{i−1}, t, x)`. The committed table is a shared slice of rows `[u64; 4]`, one per cycle (`Rv64iWitness::bits`, an `Arc<[BitsRow]>`, in `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md` §12); `Bits[y, j]` is bit `y mod 64` of word `y / 64` of row `j`, for `y < 256`. A pass borrows the slice for the length of the call.

**Sources.** A `LaneSource` gives, per cycle, the words `A`, `B`, `C` of two lane groups (`lanes(j)`, indexed `[group][A|B|C]`) and a tail byte (`tail(j)`: bits 0–1 are `A` of rows 128 and 129, bits 2–3 `B`, bits 4–5 `C`). A `CycleSource` gives, per cycle, `trace_words()` words (`trace_word(i, j)`), a bytecode row index (`bytecode_index(j) < bytecode_rows()`) and, per digit column `c`, a digit `digit(c, j)` that is `None` or below `2^bits(c)`; per bytecode row it gives `bytecode_words()` words (`bytecode_word(i, k)`). A column declared `by_row` satisfies `digit(c, j) = row_digit(c, bytecode_index(j))`.

`ValidatedTrace::prepare(source, request)` reads the source once and returns the validated source with the byte groups the request names, or a `SourceError` for the first fault: `CycleCount` or `BytecodeRows` for a count that is not a power of two, `Width` for a column whose `2^bits(c)` cannot be represented, `Column` or `GroupWidth` for a requested column that is out of range or too wide for its group, `BytecodeIndex` for a row index out of range, `Digit` or `RowDigitRange` for a digit at or above `2^bits(c)`, `RowDigit` for a `by_row` column that disagrees with its row, and `MissingDigit` for an absent digit in a column of a present group. `ValidatedTrace::new(source)` is `prepare` with an empty request. The two walks run in parallel, the rows in fixed chunks of 4,096 and the cycles in the chunks of `CycleChunks::new(log_t, 0)`; inside a chunk of cycles the walk reads the bytecode indices and then one column at a time, and allocates nothing per chunk. The fault returned is the one a serial walk in cycle order meets first, on every pool: the dimensions and the request before any digit; every row before any cycle; rows ascending and, within a row, columns ascending; cycles ascending and, within a cycle, `BytecodeIndex` and then columns ascending, with `Digit`, then `RowDigit`, then `MissingDigit` for one column. A `by_row` column reads no cache entry at an invalid bytecode index. The row caches the walks compare against hold one byte per row for a `by_row` column narrower than 8 bits and 8 bytes for one of 8 bits or more, and are dropped before `prepare` returns.

A request (`PrepareRequest`) is two lists of column lists, `present` and `optional`, and the result (`PreparedGroups`) has one group per list, in the same order. A group is one byte per cycle and column, the bytes of a cycle adjacent in the order of its list, and is written by the walk that validates the digits and by nothing else: no later pass gathers a digit into a byte. A `PresentGroup` holds digits of at most 8 bits that are present on every cycle, as themselves; an `OptionalGroup` holds digits of at most 7 bits as the digit plus one, with 0 for absence. A column may be named in several groups, or twice in one. Both types implement `ChunkIndexSource` of `jolt-kernels`, with `index_bound(c) = Some(2^bits(c))`; neither has a public constructor, so a group is evidence that its digits are in range and, for a present group, present. `ChunkProductCore` takes a present group by value and `RoutersCycleCore` an optional one, and the lazy family of each reads the group's bytes and never the source. Every other core and pass that reads digits takes the validated source. The rules are therefore checked in one place and no round loop tests a digit.

Both traits require `Send + Sync + 'static`, and a core that reads the source holds it in an `Arc`: a core is `'static`, shares the source with the other cores of a proof, and copies nothing from it except into the tables invariant 3 names. A core that reads only digits owns a prepared group and holds no source. An adapter implements the traits over the tables of the witness: the five base words of a cycle from `Rv64iWitness::words`, and a 16-byte row per cycle, decoded once from `Bits`, that holds the word `Inc` and every digit. The benchmarks implement them over a synthetic trace.

**Bitwise outer sum-check.** The row index is `y = p + 64·g` with position `p < 64` and group `g < 4`. `A[y, j]` is bit `p` of the group's `A` word for `g < 2`, the tail bit for `g = 2` and `p < 2`, and zero elsewhere; `B` and `C` likewise. For a point `τ` of `8 + log_t` coordinates `OuterF2Core` proves

`Σ_{y < 256, j < T} eq(τ, (y, j))·(A[y, j]·B[y, j] + C[y, j]) = 0`

in `8 + log_t` rounds of degree 3 and returns `A`, `B`, `C` at the challenge point. It is the core of the member `SpartanOuterF2`: the lanes are `LaneRows::values` of `specs/rv64i-binary-arithmetisation.md` (rows 0–63 the adder lane, 64–127 the AND lane) and the tail is rows 128 and 129.

**Routers.** A router has a bank of source bits, a selector that is a product of one-hot factors, and a public tensor; several routers share a space of `S` *slots*, the variables of one short sum-check. `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md` §8.4 defines the five routers of the experiment and their slots. A `RouterShape` carries the same data to a core:

- a bank: a power-of-two list of word slots, each `Trace(i)`, `Bytecode(i)`, `Bits(entries)` or `Zero`. A `Bits` slot has at most 64 entries, each `Indicator { column, value }` (the bit `digit(column, j) = Some(value)`), `DigitBit { column, bit }` (bit `bit` of the digit, zero on `None`), `One` or `Zero`. The source index of bit `i` of word slot `n` is `s = i + 64·n`;
- one to three digit columns as selector factors. A selector value `h` spells the factors' digits in mixed radix, low factor first. `RoutersCycleCore` takes factor columns of at most 7 bits, since it keeps a factor's digit and its absence in one byte; the widest of the experiment has 6;
- a slot map, which gives every variable of the shape a distinct slot below `S`. The six *bit* variables are slots 0–5, bit `i` at slot `i`, in every shape; a shape is given the slots of its word and selector variables only. The *word* variables index the word slot. The *selector* variables are the index bits of each factor. The slots a shape does not use are its *idle* slots;
- an output size `2^log_outputs` and the set `route` of triples `(o, s, h)` on which the tensor `Route` is 1.

For `u ∈ {0,1}^S`, `u|_ρ` is the restriction of `u` to the slots of shape `ρ`, split into its source part `u|src` (the bit and word slots) and its selector part `u|sel`. A shape's tables are indexed by its variables in increasing slot order, the order in which the short sum-check binds them. With that:

- `Source_ρ[s, j]` is bit `i` of word slot `n` at cycle `j`; `Select_ρ[h, j]` is 1 when every factor's digit is `Some` and they spell `h`.
- `Fold_ρ[s, h] = Σ_j eq(r_cycle, j)·Source_ρ[s, j]·Select_ρ[h, j]`, for every source index and every selector value; `W_ρ[s, h] = Σ_o eq(w, o)·Route_ρ[o, s, h]`; and `Idle_ρ(u) = Π_k (1 + u_k)` over the idle slots `k` of `ρ`.
- The **short core** is one sum-check for all shapes. It proves `Σ_{u ∈ {0,1}^S} Σ_ρ Idle_ρ(u)·W_ρ[u|_ρ]·Fold_ρ[u|_ρ]` in `S` rounds of degree 2, slot 0 first, and returns, at its challenge point `x`, each `Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)` and each `Idle_ρ(x)·W_ρ(x|_ρ)`. `r_bit = x[0..6)` is common to all shapes.
- The **cycle core** proves, for each shape, `Σ_j eq(r_cycle, j)·Source_ρ(x|src, j)·Π_f Sel_f(j) = Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)`, where `Sel_f(j) = eq(x|_f, digit(c_f, j))`, or zero on `None`, and `x|_f` is `x` on the factor's slots. Each shape is one batch member of `log_t` rounds and degree `2 + F` for `F` factors; the member returns `Source_ρ(x|src, r')` and each `Sel_f(r')` at the batch's challenge point `r'`.

The extension `Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)` depends on every entry of the table, so `Fold_ρ` is built in full. The support of `Route` enters in one place, the table `W_ρ`.

The experiment has `S = 17` and five shapes, which serve the member `RouterShort` and the five `RouterCycle` members of `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md` §8.4–8.9. That section fixes their banks and tensors, and its slot table is the definition; the table below restates it. In the `Variant` shape, word slots 0–8 are the nine words of §8.4 (`Trace` for `Rs1Value`, `Rs2Value`, `RdPreValue`, `NextPC` and `Inc`; `Bytecode` for `Imm`, `FallThroughPC`, `PCPlusImm` and `PC`), the slots from 9 are `Bits` slots that hold, from source index 576, the `n(a) + 17` indicator and flag entries of the layout and then `One` (`a = log_K_ram`, and `n` is the indicator count of the arithmetisation spec), and the slots after them, up to 15, are `Zero`. That is two `Bits` slots when `a ≤ 30`, as at the layout `(20, 20)`, where `One` is source index 668 and slots 11–15 are `Zero`, and three for every larger exponent a row admits, as at `(3, 46)`, where `One` is source index 761 and slots 12–15 are `Zero`. In `Compare`, word slot 3 is a `Bits` slot whose entry 0 is `One`. The five values `Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)` are the values sent between the two batches.

**Chunk products.** For `d` digit columns (`1 ≤ d ≤ 7`) of at most 8 bits whose digits are all `Some`, a point `a_c` of `bits(c)` coordinates per column and a weight `W` over the cycles, `ChunkProductCore` proves

`Σ_j W[j]·Π_{c<d} eq(a_c, digit(c, j))`

in `log_t` rounds of degree `d + 1` and returns `W(r')` and each `Ra_c(r') = Σ_j eq(r', j)·eq(a_c, digit(c, j))`. The weight is a `ChunkWeight`. `Dense(table)` is a table of `T` elements; `combined_weight(log_t, terms)` builds `W[j] = Σ_i κ_i·E_i(j)` from `ChunkWeightTerm`s: `Eq { coefficient, point }`, with `E(j) = eq(point, j)`, and `Next { coefficient, point }`, with `E(j) = eq(point, j − 1)` for `j ≥ 1` and `E(0) = 0`. `EqTerms(terms)` is a list of `EqTerm { coefficient, point, claim }` that stands for `Σ_i κ_i·eq(point_i, j)` without a table, where `claim_i` is the sum under the term's own weight, its coefficient included. The terms of a table and the weight of a core are two types, so that neither consumer has a variant to refuse. The core serves `BytecodeReadCycle` (five terms, one of them `Next`) and `RamRaProduct` (two terms).

**Reduction of committed claims.** For weight vectors `L_i ∈ F128^256`, `g_pass_digits(source, map, weights)` returns the tables `G_i[j] = Σ_y L_i[y]·Bits[y, j]` for at most four weight vectors, read from a `CycleSource` through a `ColumnMap`. A *leg* is a `ReductionLeg { table, point, coefficient, claim }`: a table, a cycle point `t`, a coefficient `κ` and a claim; a core has at most eight. `ReductionCore` proves `Σ_legs κ·Σ_j eq(t, j)·G[j]` in `log_t` rounds of degree 2 and returns each table at `r'`. `column_pass(rows, r)` returns `C[y] = Σ_j eq(r, j)·Bits[y, j]` for the 256 columns, so that `G_i(r') = Σ_y L_i[y]·C[y]`. They serve `BitsReduction`, whose claims at three cycle points have weights that are non-zero on 21, 20 and 8 byte positions of a row, and the vector `C` of batch 6b.

### Invariants

1. **Definitional messages.** Every message of a core is the round polynomial of its definition, and every final value is the multilinear extension of its table at the challenge point. A table that a definition names is built on its whole domain: `fold_pass` computes `Fold_ρ[s, h]` for every `s` and `h` and does not read `route`.
2. **Characteristic 2.** No kernel samples a round polynomial at an integer, halves, scales by a power of two, or calls `from_u64`, `from_i128` or an integer-scalar `fmadd_*`. Constants are `F128::from_raw` values; multiplication by `F128::from_raw(2)` is `mul_x`.
3. **Representation.** Inputs are shared packed words; no kernel stores a field element per bit. A table of `F128` exists only where a variable ranging over bits has been bound or summed out: the outer core holds 6 elements and one byte per cycle from round 7; `source_lift` holds one element per trace word and cycle until `claims_pass`, and one per shape and cycle for `Source_ρ(x|src, ·)`; a selector or chunk column becomes dense at `T/16` after its fourth bind; a chunk product holds its weight when the weight is `Dense`; the reduction holds one element per table and cycle. Two buffers are not of field elements: `ChunkProductCore` and `RoutersCycleCore` each own a prepared group, one byte per selected column and cycle, from construction until their columns become dense (Sources; Design, "Chunk products" and "Routers").
4. **Allocation and scratch.** No heap allocation per cycle, per pair or per chunk. Tables are allocated at construction or at one of the phase changes named in the Design and bound between two alternating buffers. Bucket arrays and histograms come from a `ScratchPool`, so a pass holds at most one layout per worker thread, whatever its number of chunks; a scatter holds one buffer of `T` weights and no table per worker. A chunk body that holds an array of a `ScratchPool` is sequential: until it has returned the array it starts no Rayon work (`join`, `scope`, a parallel iterator) and does not yield. Every table of `T` or `bytecode_rows()` elements has one owner and the drop point the Performance section lists.
5. **Determinism.** Messages and final values do not depend on the thread count or on the order in which a batch visits its members. Chunks are unions of whole split-equality blocks, of a length that `CycleChunks` fixes from `log_t` and the round; partial results combine by XOR (`F128Accumulator::merge`).
6. **Honest inputs.** The cores take preconditions they do not check. `OuterF2Core` requires `C = A & B` on every lane word and on the tail bits. `ReductionCore` requires each leg's claim to equal `Σ_j eq(t, j)·G[j]`. `ChunkProductCore` with `EqTerms` requires each term's claim to equal the sum under that term's weight. The routers take three more: `RouterShortCore` requires each `Fold_ρ` it is given to be the complete fold of the source, `RoutersCycleCore` requires each source table to be `Source_ρ(x|src, ·)`, and each of its members requires its input claim to be the sum it proves, because the member recovers `q(1)` from that claim. On an input that violates one, the messages belong to another polynomial and the verifier rejects at its final check. `check_rows` reports the first violating cycle and `check_claims` the first violating leg; they are diagnostics a caller may run, and no acceptance criterion rests on them.
7. **Tables in cache.** The lookup tables one pass reads per thread total at most 96 KiB, except: the buckets of `fold_pass` (11.5 MiB per worker in the default layout); the tables indexed by bytecode row; the byte buckets of `column_pass` (128.5 KiB per worker); the window tables of `OuterF2Core` under `folded_group_weights` (112 KiB); and the branch tables of a chunk product whose columns are wider than 4 bits, which reach `16·2^bits(c)` elements per column at the fourth bind (4 KiB for a column of 4 bits, 64 KiB for one of 8). Seven columns of 4 bits stay within the bound, and those are the columns of the protocol's chunk products.
8. **Total functions.** Constructors and passes validate lengths, slots, indices and digit ranges and return a `thiserror` enum, one per module, naming the offending length, index, slot, column, cycle or digit; nothing panics on malformed input. Each promise of this kind has a negative case in the acceptance criteria. What a constructor cannot see is a call sequence: the indices passed to `LazyFoldedRa::value` and the number of calls to `bind` are preconditions of that type, stated by `specs/binary-kernel-primitives.md`, and a core meets them by construction, reading below the current length and binding once per cycle round. The crate has `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`; the counting allocator of the benchmarks is in `benches/support/`, outside the library.
9. **Upstream changes preserve behaviour.** The changes to `jolt-field`, `jolt-poly` and `jolt-kernels` are those of `specs/binary-kernel-primitives.md`, whose first invariant states what they leave unchanged. This spec adds none.
10. **Boundary.** The crate depends on `jolt-field` (feature `binary`), `jolt-poly`, `jolt-sumcheck`, `jolt-kernels`, `jolt-utils` (the zero-page allocation of trace-sized buffers), `rayon` and `thiserror`, and on `rand_chacha` under `test-utils`. `jolt-transcript` is a dev-dependency: the tests and the benchmarks need a transcript for `prove_batch` and for the verifier, and the library does not. The crate depends on no relation type and on no arithmetisation crate; a row of the committed table is `[u64; 4]`.
11. **No protocol change.** No core, option or variant of this spec changes a message, a round count, a member or the transcript of `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md`.
12. **One variable order.** Every point is low variable first (Notation). The reversal that the helpers of `jolt-poly` need is written once, in `round::eq`. No other module reverses a point, calls `EqPolynomial::evals` or constructs a `GruenSplitEqPolynomial`, and no code reverses the challenge list of `prove_batch`.

No `jolt-eval` invariant changes: nothing here is reachable from the existing prover.

### Non-Goals

Implementations of `SumcheckKernel` and `PrepareKernel` for the relations of `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md`, and their acceptance against the reference kernels of that spec, which the Design states; the decode of the committed table into digits; the routing tensors and the public addends of a router's outputs; the cores of `SpartanOuterF128`, `SpartanInner`, the three members of batch 4, the two of batch 5 and `BytecodeReadAddress`, which follow on the same machinery and runner; a univariate-skip first round; the two prover-only candidates for the routers and the bitmap skip listed under Alternatives; any change to `prove_batch`; witness generation; any commitment.

## Evaluation

### Acceptance Criteria

"By summation" means computed from the definitions in Goal with `oracle`, on tables built in the test. "Rejected" means that the prover returns `SumcheckError::RoundCheckFailed` or that the verifier of `jolt-sumcheck` rejects, at whichever check fails. A core takes its value at 1 from the claim, so a proof made for a wrong claim satisfies every round check in which that value is recovered by division. It is caught in a round in which the value is recovered by evaluation (`l(1) = 0`), where the core returns the actual sum as the error, or at the verifier's final check, which a wrong claim passes with probability at most the soundness error of the sum-check, the sum of the round degrees over `2^128`. The seeds are fixed, so the outcome of each rejection test is determined.

- [ ] **Shared crates.** The acceptance criteria of `specs/binary-kernel-primitives.md` hold on the branch this crate builds on. The prepared groups are the sources of the lazy families of this crate: their bytes are pinned against literal fixtures (`tests/sources.rs`), and the criteria "Chunk products" and "Routers" read every value of a family through them.
- [ ] **Variable order.** For the point `t = (0, 1)`, low variable first, and `G = [0, 1, 0, 1]`: `oracle::mle_at(G, t) = 0`; `eq_table(t, None) = [0, 0, 1, 0]`, which selects index 2, so that `Σ_j eq(t, j)·G[j] = 0`; `split_eq(t, None).current_linear_evals() = (1, 0)`, the factor `1 + X`; and the first message of a `ReductionCore` with the one leg `(G, t, κ = 1, claim 0)` is `X + X^2`. The same test pins the convention of the helpers it converts for: `EqPolynomial::evals(&[0, 1], None) = [0, 1, 0, 0]`, which selects index 1 and gives the sum 1, and `GruenSplitEqPolynomial::new(&[0, 1], LowToHigh)` has the factor `X`. Every seeded point of the criteria below has pairwise distinct coordinates; a point with all coordinates equal does not tell the two orders apart.
- [ ] **Lifts, buckets, nodes.** `WordLift` and `NibbleLift` equal `Σ_i w[i]·ω_i` on the unit words, `u64::MAX` and 10,000 seeded words. `NibbleBuckets`, `ByteBuckets` and `DigitHistogram` equal the per-bit and per-digit sums of a seeded sequence, and two merged halves equal the whole. For degrees 2 to 8, `coefficients_from_nodes` recovers a seeded polynomial from its leading coefficient and its values at 0, 1 and the nodes, and returns `degree + 1` coefficients. `quadratic_at_nodes` and `linear_at_nodes` equal the values of a seeded factor at `F128::from_raw(2)` to `F128::from_raw(7)` computed with field multiplications.

### Testing Strategy

The ground truth is `oracle`: it expands each leaf to a dense vector, binds by `f(r) = f_0 + r·(f_0 + f_1)`, evaluates the summand at `d + 1` distinct field elements and interpolates with `interpolate_nodes_to_coeffs` (`jolt-poly/src/lagrange.rs`). It shares no table, lift or round assembly with the cores, and it calls no equality helper: `mle_at` binds the low variable first by its own loop. It is therefore the ground truth for the variable order as well, the literal of the "Variable order" criterion is checked against it, and the seeded points have pairwise distinct coordinates, since a point of equal coordinates, all zero or all one included, cannot tell the two orders apart. Verification in the tests goes through `prove_batch` and the verifier of `jolt-sumcheck` over `BooleanHypercube`.

Each test asserts an algebraic identity against that ground truth, a rejection, or a typed error, clauses (c) and (e) of `.claude/skills/test-policy/SKILL.md`; none compares one configuration of a core with another, and every option and layout is tested against the same summation. A rejection test asserts that the prover returns `RoundCheckFailed` or that the verifier rejects; it does not assert the round at which either happens. A test of the "Rejected inputs" criterion asserts the variant and the fields that name the value, one case per rule, and does not repeat the unit tests of a helper. Seeds are fixed. The existing tests of `jolt-field`, `jolt-poly`, `jolt-sumcheck` and `jolt-kernels` pass unchanged. The `host` and `zk` modes do not apply.

Rustdoc on the public items states the contracts of the surface table and, on `OuterF2Core::new`, `ReductionLeg` and `EqTerm`, invariant 6 in those terms: required of the caller, not checked, detected by the verifier's final check of the batched statement. That check does not reach a leg whose coefficient is zero or legs at one point whose errors cancel; `check_claims` names the individual leg. Comments in the round loops are limited to what `.claude/skills/comment-policy/SKILL.md` keeps: the identity a formula implements where the code cannot show it, and the coupling of a variable order to the definitions in this spec.

### Performance

Benchmarks run on an Apple M4 Max with `-C target-cpu=native`, on one thread and on a `rayon` pool of 12 threads, at `log_t = 20` and `log_t = 22`, with `2^20` bytecode rows and `2^20` RAM words at both lengths. Every benchmark goes through one runner in `benches/support/`, whose one sampling loop is `run_prepared_cases`. A bench gives it a function from the synthetic source to a prepared fixture (the validated trace, plans, input claims), a function from the fixture to the input a sample consumes, and a list of cases, each with its named phases and the phases that make its total. The runner builds and warms the pool of a configuration before the first interval, prepares the fixture once per source and size outside every timed interval and every allocation interval and reports its cost as its own record, runs the cases of one source in rotation (A B C A B C) with a fresh kernel per sample, makes the consumed input of a sample (a prepared group, which a core takes by value) in a timed and counted interval of its own before the core's and reports it as the record `<case>_preparation`, outside the four phases, releases measured state and fixtures only after their counters have stopped, and alone computes the minimum, median and maximum of each phase time and of the per-sample total, the comparison with a threshold and the comparison of two variants. A result above its threshold is printed as such and does not fail the process: the acceptance criterion is applied to the output. The total is the median of the per-sample sums and not the sum of the phase medians. No bench of a core or pass reads a clock itself: one that times a part of its own work takes `Clock` from `support`. The unit benches `probe` and `machinery` keep their own entries, `run_probe` and `run_machinery`, in the same module, over the same sampling loop. `round` is an independent unit bench of the round arithmetic with a loop of its own, and `chunk_product` keeps the fitted growth model of its allocation check. `run_cases` is the same loop for cases that consume nothing, and `run_core` and `run_batch` are layers over the two with the four phases of a core: construction with the passes that feed it; the rounds, under challenges from a fixed seed for one core and under the transcript's for a batch through `prove_batch`; `finish_rounds`; and the extraction of the final values with the passes that follow the rounds. Under a counting allocator the runner reports the peak and the final live bytes above the baseline of the interval, which leaves out the resident source, the fixture and the pools. Ids are `<bench>/<profile>/<log_t>/<threads>`.

`SyntheticTrace` is a function of a `SynthProfile` and a seed (`ChaCha20Rng`). Its rows carry the chunk indicators at stated column ranges, its digits agree with them, and its lanes satisfy `C = A & B`.

## Design

### Architecture

**Round messages.** A core returns `UnivariatePoly::new(coefficients)`; `prove_batch` and `BooleanHypercube::round_sum_coefficients` work on coefficients, and the proof omits the linear one (`specs/binary-sumcheck.md`). Internally a polynomial of degree `d` is known by its value at 0, its leading coefficient (the value "at infinity"), its value at 1 from the running claim, and its values at the `d − 2` nodes `F128::from_raw(2)`, …, `F128::from_raw(d − 1)`, for `d` up to 8. `coefficients_from_nodes` subtracts the leading term, interpolates with a matrix fixed per degree and returns exactly `d + 1` coefficients, so that the result is a message, or the argument of `round_poly_from_q_coeffs`, as it stands.

No node costs a field multiplication, and the values of one factor at all the nodes share their shifts. Write `X` for the generator, so that the nodes 2 to 7 are `X`, `X + 1`, `X^2`, `X^2 + 1`, `X^2 + X` and `X^2 + X + 1`. For a quadratic `q(t) = a + b·t + c·t^2`: `q(2) = a + X·(b + X·c)` and `q(4) = a + X^2·(b + X^2·c)`, which take five `mul_x`, `X·c` being shared, and two when only nodes 2 and 3 are read; then `q(3) = q(2) + b + c`, `q(5) = q(4) + b + c`, `q(6) = q(2) + q(4) + a` and `q(7) = q(6) + b + c`, by additions alone, because squaring is additive. `quadratic_at_nodes` returns the six values and is the one owner of these identities; `linear_at_nodes` is the same with `c = 0` and two shifts. The counts of Performance price a quadratic at six shifts, each product of `X·b`, `X^2·b`, `X·c`, `X^2·c`, `X^3·c`, `X^4·c` formed apart, and are upper bounds by that one shift. A core that needs fewer nodes reads a prefix, and fixes how many outside its loop over the pairs, so that the compiler drops the rest. `eval_at_node` evaluates a polynomial of any degree at one node by Horner's rule with the same shifts; it is for a value per round, not for a loop over pairs, where a node that is a loop variable costs a branch per bit of the node. Products of linear factors are formed in pairs: `quadratic` returns the three coefficients of a product of two by Karatsuba (3 multiplications), `quadratic_at_nodes` gives its values, and the pointwise products follow.

Under an equality weight the round polynomial is `l(t)·q(t)` with `l(t) = σ·(1 + w + t)`, where `w` is the weight's coordinate for the round and `σ` the product of the bound coordinates' factors. A kernel accumulates `q(0)` and the higher values of `q`, obtains `q(1) = (claim + l(0)·q(0))/l(1)` from `recover_q_one`, interpolates `q` and multiplies by `l` with `round_poly_from_q_coeffs`. `l(1) = σ·w` is zero in two cases, and in both the closure evaluates `q(1)` by the same loop on the other half of each pair. When `w = 0`, `l` is `σ·(1 + t)` and the claim is `σ·q(0)`. When `σ = 0`, which a bind at `r = 1 + w` of an earlier round causes, `l` is zero, the message is zero whatever `q` is, the claim is zero for an honest prover, and the rounds continue: the tables are still bound and `q` is still non-zero in general. In either case a claim that disagrees is `Err` of the sum the tables give, which a core returns as `SumcheckError::RoundCheckFailed`. `gruen_poly_deg_3` and `gruen_poly_from_evals` evaluate `l` at 2 and 3 as integers and stay as they are for prime fields.

**Lifts and buckets.** A sum of bits against weights is a linear map of a word, so `WordLift` is eight tables of 256 entries (32 KiB) and a lift is eight lookups and seven XORs; `NibbleLift` uses 16-entry tables for words compacted by `gather`. Scalars that multiply a lift (a challenge monomial, a row weight) are folded into its tables. In the other direction, the sums `Σ_j e_j·bit_i(j)` for many bits `i` of a word are collected by `bucket[position][value] ^= e_j`, one load-XOR-store per nibble or byte per cycle, and read out at the end as XORs of the buckets whose index has bit `i`. `DigitHistogram` does the same for a digit column with one XOR per cycle. Neither direction multiplies in the field per bit.

*Placement.* A processor compares the low 12 bits of a load's address with those of the stores still in flight, and holds the load back on a match until both addresses are known. A pass that XORs one `e_j` into several tables at the same value therefore must not place those tables a multiple of 4,096 bytes apart, and words do take the same value in many places at once: a word of a program is mostly zero bytes. With one table of 256 elements per byte position, 4,096 bytes each, every position of the column pass matched every other at an equal value, and on a probe with 85% zero bytes an update cost 7.8 ns on a Ryzen 9950X and 1.0 ns on an M4 against 0.4 and 0.3 with the tables 16 bytes further apart; uniform bytes showed no difference. `BucketPlacement` therefore leaves one unused element after every 256: a byte position is 257 elements from the last, and a nibble word, sixteen positions of 16, is 257 elements from the last word. Two byte entries then match exactly when `Δposition + Δvalue ≡ 0 (mod 256)` and two nibble entries when `Δword + 16·Δposition + Δvalue ≡ 0 (mod 256)`, and at an equal value neither holds within the 32 positions of the column pass, within the byte words of one selector value (at most 72 positions) or within its nibble words (at most nine). That is the whole of the law, and one test checks it on the offsets the passes use. It does not cover the tables of two shapes that one cycle writes, nor a word set against the digit tables of its shape: a nibble word occupies one of the sixteen residue classes of its base modulo 16 elements, so only a layout that assigned the classes across all the shapes at once could, and `FoldLayout` does not. In the layout with no byte buckets two such coincidences were found among the tables one cycle can write: a word set of the 16-slot shape against one of the shift shape, and against a digit table. Unequal values can match in either form. The cost is 32 elements of the column pass's scratch and 0.4% of a fold layout.

**Equality over cycles.** The two equality helpers of `jolt-poly` index by the high variable first: entry `k` of `EqPolynomial::evals(r, _)` pairs `r[0]` with the top bit of `k`, and a `GruenSplitEqPolynomial` under `LowToHigh` reads the last coordinate of its point in the first round. `round::eq` is the one module that calls them, and its private `msb_first(point)`, which returns the coordinates in reverse, is the one place the order is converted. It exports two functions on points written as in Notation:

- `eq_table(point, scale)` is `EqPolynomial::evals(&msb_first(point), scale)`: entry `k` is `scale·eq(point, k)`, with bit `i` of `k` on coordinate `i`.
- `split_eq(point, scale)` is `GruenSplitEqPolynomial::new_with_scaling(&msb_first(point), LowToHigh, scale)`: round `i` consumes coordinate `i`, the inner table is indexed by the low bits of the pair index and the outer one by the high bits. It returns `RoundError::EmptyPoint` for a point of no coordinates: the constructor accepts one, and `current_linear_evals` then has no coordinate to read.

`scale` is an `Option`, `None` standing for 1, as in the two helpers.

No other module calls `EqPolynomial::evals` or a constructor of `GruenSplitEqPolynomial`, no kernel reverses a point, and no challenge list is reversed: the list `prove_batch` returns is low first already. The digit tables `eq(a_c, ·)` and `eq(x|_f, ·)` of the lazy columns, the half tables of every pass and the tables of every `Eq` term are `eq_table`s; every equality weight of a cycle round is a `split_eq`.

Passes and the outer row rounds use two half tables over the cycle variables: `CycleChunks::split_point` cuts the point into its low and high coordinates, `E_lo` and `E_hi` are the `eq_table`s of the two, and `e_j = E_hi[j_hi]·E_lo[j_lo]`. A sum of per-cycle values `v_j` is `inner.fmadd(E_lo[j_lo], v_j)` in an `F128Accumulator`, reduced and multiplied by `E_hi[j_hi]` once per block; a bucket pass forms `e_j` with one multiplication. Cycle rounds use the methods of `GruenSplitEqPolynomial` (`current_linear_evals`, `e_in_current`, `e_out_current`, `bind`) unchanged. Parallel loops are `par_chunks` over whole `E_hi` blocks, with a chunk length that depends on `log_t` and the round only.

**Scratch and scatters.** Accumulators live on the stack of a chunk. Bucket arrays and histograms come from a `ScratchPool`: a chunk takes an array when it starts and returns it when it ends, an array is zeroed when it is created and keeps accumulating across the chunks that use it, and at most `rayon::current_num_threads()` arrays exist. After the pass the arrays are merged pairwise in a tree, in parallel over disjoint ranges. Peak scratch is therefore the number of workers times the layout, whatever the number of chunks, and a layout of `n` elements costs `(2W − 1)·n` element operations for zero-fill and merge on `W` workers.

That bound holds only if a worker that holds an array cannot start a second chunk of the same pass, which is what a nested `join`, `scope` or parallel iterator, or a yield, would let it do. A chunk body is therefore sequential from `take()` to the return of the array (invariant 4). The pool enforces the cap without blocking: `take()` on a pool that has lent `rayon::current_num_threads()` arrays returns `PoolError::Exhausted` and allocates nothing, and the pass returns the error. A cap that waited for an array could wait for one held further down its own stack. `merge()` empties the pool, so a pass that reuses a pool starts from zero.

A scatter by bytecode row uses no table per worker, which would be `bytecode_rows()` elements each. The rows are split into 256 ranges of equal length, and the cycles into the chunks of `CycleChunks` at round 0. Which range a cycle's row falls in, and therefore where its weight belongs among the weights of its chunk once they are grouped by range, depends on the source alone, and so does the row a grouped weight is added to. `ScatterPlan::new(source)` computes both once, in one counting pass and one placing pass over the bytecode indices: per cycle, the *slot* of its weight within its chunk, and per slot, the offset of its row within its range, each a `u16`, with the count of every chunk and range. A scatter then reads no index from the source and keeps no cursor. Its emission, parallel over chunks, stores `weight(j)` at the slot of `j` in the chunk's segment of one buffer of `T` weights (16 bytes per cycle); its application, parallel over ranges, adds each chunk's weights of the range to the range's slice of the output at the stored offsets. There is no merge, a chunk's segment and a range's slice of the output stay in cache while they are written, and the plan serves every scatter over the same source. The two widths are limits and not assumptions: a range holds at most `2^16` rows because the layout of the arithmetisation admits at most `2^24`, a chunk holds at most `2^16` cycles up to `log_t = 32`, and `new` returns a typed error beyond either. The plan is built in the construction phase of the stage that first scatters and costs 4 bytes per cycle and at most 8 per chunk and range.

**When bits become field elements.** A variable that ranges over the bits of a word (a lane position, a bit of a source word, a column of the committed table) is bound or summed out before any cycle variable, so that the first dense table holds one element per cycle and word. Until then the kernel reads words. A digit column stays a column of digits through three cycle binds (`LazyFoldedRa`) and is dense from the fourth.

#### Bitwise outer sum-check

Write `r_1, …, r_6` for the position challenges, `ρ_k(v)` for the equality weight of the position variables after the round variable at window pair `v`, and `ω_g` for the weight of group `g`. After `k` position rounds a *window* is `2^k` adjacent positions and `A_k(u) = Σ_i eq((r_1..r_k), i)·a[2^k·u + i]`; round `k + 1` needs, per pair of windows `(2v, 2v + 1)`,

`q(0) ∋ ρ_k(v)·(A_k(2v)·B_k(2v) + C_k(2v))` and `q(∞) ∋ ρ_k(v)·ΔA_k(v)·ΔB_k(v)`, with `ΔA_k(v) = A_k(2v) + A_k(2v + 1)`.

*Rounds 1 to `monomial_rounds` (default 3): products of bits as AND.* Let `μ(a)` be `moebius` of the lane word along its low `k + 1` position bits. For `S ⊆ {1..k}`, read as a number below `2^k`, bit `2^{k+1}·v + S` of `μ(a)` is the coefficient of `r^S` in `A_k(2v)`, and bit `2^{k+1}·v + S + 2^k` its coefficient in `ΔA_k(v)`. For an exponent vector `e ∈ {0,1,2}^k`, let `X_e` be the XOR of `μ(a)[S + 2^k] & μ(b)[S' + 2^k]` over the pairs of subsets whose indicator vectors sum to `e`, and `Y_e` the same without the `2^k`, each a word with one significant bit per window pair. Then `ΔA_k·ΔB_k = Σ_e r^e·X_e` (`3^k` words, `4^k` ANDs), and, because `A_k·B_k + C_k` has degree at most 2 in each `r_m` and vanishes on `{0,1}^k` when the rows are satisfied, `A_k(2v)·B_k(2v) + C_k(2v) = Σ_e (r^e + r^{red(e)})·Y_e`, where `red` replaces each 2 by 1 and only the `3^k − 2^k` vectors containing a 2 contribute (`4^k − 3^k` ANDs). The `C` lane is not read. In round 1 this gives `q(0) = q(1) = 0` and one word per group. Each word is compacted by `gather` and lifted by byte in rounds 1 and 2 and by nibble in round 3 (`OuterF2Options::nibble_round_2` moves round 2 to nibbles), with tables holding the word's scalar (`r^e`, or `r^e + r^{red(e)}`), `ρ_k(v)` and `ω_g`; the two per-cycle values go to `q(0)` and `q(∞)` with one `fmadd` each.

*Rounds up to 6: window lifts.* For `k ≥ 3` a window is `2^{k−3}` bytes, so every window value of a word is the XOR of byte lookups, eight per word whatever `k` is. The tables for `A` carry `ρ_k(v)` of the byte's window pair (8 tables), those for `C` likewise on the even windows (4 tables), those for `B` are unweighted (`2^{k−3}` tables). By default (`OuterF2Options::folded_group_weights`) the `A` and `C` tables carry `ω_g` as well, one set per group and 112 KiB in all, and one accumulator pair serves the cycle: per window pair and group, `acc0.fmadd(A(2v), B(2v))`, `acc0.add(C(2v))` and `accinf.fmadd(ΔA, ΔB)`; per cycle the two accumulators are reduced and multiplied by `E_lo[j_lo]`. Without the option the groups share one set of `A` and `C` tables, 64 KiB in all, each group has its own accumulator pair, reduced per cycle, and `ω_g` multiplies each group's total once per round: six reductions and six `fmadd` more per cycle over rounds 4 to 6. The dimensions of a round in either form (table length, tables per word, monomial counts, windows per unit) have one owner, a constant function of the round index, and the statically dispatched passes take their constants from it.

*The switch.* Per group and round the monomial form lifts `2·3^k − 2^k` words, and the window form makes 20 byte lookups (40 nibble lookups below `k = 3`) and `2^{6−k}` multiply-accumulates. At price point 1 and `k = 2` (round 3) the first costs 37 ns per cycle and the second 74; at `k = 3` (round 4) they cost 76 and 41, and the order is the same at price point 2. `OuterF2Options::monomial_rounds` is in `2..=6` so that both forms are tested at rounds 3 to 6; at 2 the window of round 3 is a nibble and its 16 tables per lane have 16 entries. The benchmark of the core, on a loaded machine, has the switch after round 2 faster than the default by 4% at both sizes, inside the spread of its samples and against a model that prices it 34 ns dearer: the default stays 3 until a quiet measurement separates them.

*The tail.* Rows 128 and 129 are positions 0 and 1 of group 2. The pass of round 1 builds `H[i] = Σ_j e_j·[tail(j) = i]` for the 64 tail values with one XOR per cycle into a per-block histogram. The tail's terms of rounds 1 to 7 are sums over those 64 values of `H[i]` times the summand evaluated on the six bits of `i`.

*Rounds 7 and 8.* After round 6 the core materialises `A`, `B`, `C` of groups 0 and 1 at `(r_1..r_6)` with one `WordLift` (48 lookups, 96 bytes per cycle) and keeps the tail byte; the tail's values are lookups in three 64-entry tables. Round 7 pairs group 0 with group 1 on the tables and group 2 with the empty group 3 on `H`. Round 8 is per cycle: its low half is the bound pair of groups 0 and 1 and its high half is `(1 + r_7)` times the tail lookup. The binds at `r_7` and `r_8` are fused into the next round's pass and written in place.

*Cycle rounds.* Three tables of `T` elements under `split_eq(τ_cycle, Some(σ))`, `τ_cycle` being the last `log_t` coordinates of `τ` in the order of Notation and `σ` the product over the eight row rounds. Per pair: `q(0) = A_0·B_0 + C_0`, `q(∞) = ΔA·ΔB`, each multiplied into the split-equality accumulators, and three binds into the second buffers, which are the freed tables of group 1.

#### Routers

*What the slots share.* Bit variable `i` is slot `i` in every shape, so one point `r_bit` serves every source word: `source_lift` builds one `WordLift` and lifts each trace word once for all shapes, and every word value that `claims_pass` returns is at `(r_bit, r')`. Two shapes that give the same digit column the same slots have the same factor table `Sel_f`: the cycle core holds it once. In the experiment the low digit factor at slots 6–8 belongs to three shapes and the high digit factor at slots 9–11 to two, so the five members bind 8 selector columns for their 11 factors, and the two shapes with both digit factors form the product of the two columns once per pair. The slots do not reduce the bucket work of `fold_pass`: a shape's buckets are keyed by its own selector value, so a word in two banks is bucketed once per shape.

*`fold_pass(source, shapes, r_cycle, plan, layout, histogram_columns)`* makes one parallel pass with `e_j = eq(r_cycle, j)` and builds every entry of every `Fold_ρ`. For each shape and each cycle whose factor digits are all `Some`, spelling `h`, it XORs `e_j` under the key `h` into one bucket per nibble of every `Trace` slot of the shape, 16 per word, and into one bucket per digit column that a `Bits` entry of the shape names. The same pass scatters `ra_fold[bytecode_index(j)] ^= e_j` through `plan`. A `Bytecode` slot of a shape whose factors are all `by_row` is not bucketed per cycle: a pass over the rows with a non-zero entry of `ra_fold` XORs that entry into the nibble buckets of the row's words under the row's `h`. A `Bytecode` slot of any other shape is bucketed per cycle like a `Trace` slot. A `One` entry under `h` is the sum of `e_j` over the cycles that spell `h`. Where the pass buckets a word slot of the shape under `h`, per cycle or per row, that sum is the total of the 16 buckets of one nibble position of the word. A digit bucket cannot give it, because a cycle whose digit is `None` is in no digit bucket. A shape with no `Trace` and no `Bytecode` slot therefore keeps one total per selector value, at one XOR per cycle that spells a value; the minimal bank `[Bits([One])]` is such a shape, and none of the five shapes of the experiment is. A requested histogram is a marginal where one exists: over `h`, of the digit buckets of a shape whose selector is present on every cycle; over the visited rows, of `ra_fold`, for a `by_row` column; otherwise it costs one more XOR per cycle. The read-out forms the entry of a source bit as the XOR of the 8 buckets of its nibble whose index has the bit. The only entries that cost nothing are those no cycle reaches: `Zero` slots and entries, and selector values no cycle spells.

`layout` is a `FoldLayout`: per shape, the selector values whose word buckets are by byte, 256 per byte position and 8 XORs per word. `selector_counts(source, shape)` returns the number of cycles per selector value, and `FoldLayout::byte_bucket_values(counts, limit)` picks the `limit` most frequent, the lower value first among equal counts, so that a layout is a function of the trace. Bucket arrays come from a `ScratchPool`. The outputs are the dense `Fold_ρ` of each shape, indexed in the shape's slot order, `ra_fold` and the histograms. For the five shapes of the experiment the pass costs, per cycle, one multiplication, one scatter and `88 + 16·p_shift + 32·p_access + 48·p_key + 32·p_taken` bucket XORs, with `p` the share of cycles that have a shift kind, a RAM access, a key kind, or are a taken branch; per visited row it costs 64 bucket XORs for the four `Bytecode` words of the 16-slot shape.

*`RouterShortCore::new(shapes, w, folds)`* scatters `eq(w, o)` over each `route` set into a dense `W_ρ` and runs one sum-check of `S` rounds. Each shape keeps its pair of tables, its current sum `S_ρ` and a scalar `λ_ρ`, initially 1. In round `k` a shape that has slot `k` contributes `λ_ρ` times the degree-2 round polynomial of `Σ W_ρ·Fold_ρ` in its next variable, and binds both tables at the challenge; a shape that does not have slot `k` contributes `λ_ρ·(1 + X)·S_ρ` and multiplies `λ_ρ` by `1 + x_k`. The member's message is the sum of the contributions. After the last round `Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)` is the shape's one remaining `Fold` entry and `λ_ρ` times its remaining `W` entry is `Idle_ρ(x)·W_ρ(x|_ρ)`.

*`source_lift(source, shapes, x)`* builds `Source_ρ(x|src, ·)` for every shape in one pass. Each distinct trace word is lifted once at `r_bit` with one `WordLift`, and the lifts are kept, one element per trace word and cycle. A shape's value is the combination of its slots' lifts under the equality weights of its word variables, by `fmadd` and one reduction, plus one lookup per digit column named by a `Bits` slot, in a table of that column's entries under their weights, plus one read of a per-row table that holds the same combination of the shape's `Bytecode` slots, plus the shape's constant `c_ρ = Σ eq(x|src, s)` over the source indices `s` of its `One` entries. The constant is computed once per shape. Where the shape has a per-row table it is added to every entry of that table, which is the case of the two shapes of the experiment that have `One` entries; otherwise it is added to each cycle's value, one XOR. It is not added to a digit table, since a cycle whose digit is `None` makes no lookup there. It costs no multiplication and is in no count of Performance. The per-row tables are built first, in parallel over the bytecode rows, and dropped when the pass returns. The pass has two subphases that the benchmark reports apart: the row tables, `32 L + 6 A + 2 R + 1 M` per visited row, and the cycles, `59 L + 9 A + 3 R` each.

*`RoutersCycleCore::new(group, shapes, r_cycle, x, source_tables)`* owns the tables of `source_lift`, a second buffer of `T/2` for each, and one `LazyFoldedRa` over the distinct factor columns with tables `eq_table(x|_f, None)`, under one `split_eq(r_cycle, None)`. Two shapes that name the same column with the same slots share one column of the family. The family reads the digits from the optional group the constructor is given, one byte per cycle and distinct factor holding the digit plus one, with zero for `None`; that encoding is what bounds a factor column at 7 bits, and the group is released after the fourth bind. `RoutersCycleCore::columns(shapes)` is the one statement of the order of the distinct factors: the caller requests that list as one optional group, and `new` refuses a group whose columns or widths are others. `members()` returns one `RouterCycleMember` per shape; each implements `ProveRounds<F128>` and holds the core behind a shared lock that is taken once per member and round. The first member the engine calls in a round binds the previous challenge and makes the one pass of that round, accumulating the values of `q` for every shape; each member then recovers `q(1)` from its own claim and returns its own message. The recovery divides by the value at 1 of the round's equality factor. In a round where that value is zero (`r_cycle` has the coordinate 0 there, or an earlier challenge has made the scalar of the equality zero) the pass also accumulates `q(1)` itself, the product of the high sides, at one multiplication and one `fmadd` more per kept pair and member, and the member takes it from there. `finish_rounds` is the same kind of shared transition and happens once: the engine calls it on every handle it drives, the first call applies the last bind and records the final values of every member, and the others find it applied and do nothing; the source tables are released by that first call. Which handle is first does not matter, in a round or at the end. A member's messages and final values do not depend on which other members are driven: a batch may drive any non-empty subset of the handles, drop the others or keep them undriven, and read a member's final values after that member's own `finish_rounds`. A handle that is given a challenge other than the one the round was computed with is refused and may be called again with the right one. The pass works on at most eight shapes at a time from arrays on the stack, and reads each pair's selector values once for all of them when the family has at most eight columns; a larger set of shapes repeats the reads per batch of eight, and a larger family reads per factor. The five shapes of the experiment have eight distinct factors among their eleven. With one factor `q` has degree 2 and is known from 0 and infinity (2 multiplications and 2 `fmadd` per pair); with two factors it needs node 2 as well (6 and 3); with three, the source and the last factor form one quadratic, the first two factors another, and the nodes are 0, infinity, 2 and 3 (10 and 4; where two shapes have the same first two factors, the 3 multiplications of that quadratic are spent once for both). The bind of a source table is fused into the next round's pass. During the lazy rounds a pair is skipped, as contributing zero, when one of the shape's factors is zero on both of its sides. The test is on the two values the round has gathered for the pair and reads no digit: a factor whose sides are both zero is the zero polynomial in the round's variable, which is the case when every cycle of the pair's block has the digit `None` in the factor's column, and also when the two sides vanish by value. One side zero is not enough to skip. With two cycles, one factor of zero index bits, digits `[None, Some(0)]` and source `[1, 0]`, the low side of the selector is 0 and the high side 1, the summand of the pair is `X·(1 + X) = X + X^2`, and the pair is kept.

*`claims_pass(source, lifts, words, plan, r')`* runs once after the cycle rounds. With `e'_j = eq(r', j)` it returns each listed trace word at `(r_bit, r')` (one `fmadd` per word and cycle on the kept lifts), the scatter of `e'_j` by bytecode row, through `plan`, and each bytecode word at `(r_bit, r')` as a sum over the rows with a non-zero entry of that scatter. With the members' selector values and source values these are the values a stage needs at `r'`, and no bound table per word is kept through the cycle rounds.

#### Chunk products

`ChunkProductCore::new(group, points, weight)` takes a `PresentGroup` of its `d` columns by value and holds a `Dense` weight with a second buffer of `T/2` and a `LazyFoldedRa` over the `d` columns with tables `eq_table(a_c, None)`. The group is the source of the lazy family, and the constructor reads none of its digits: the four lazy rounds and the materialisation read `d` adjacent bytes per cycle and never return to the trace, a lookup has no presence test, and the core does not depend on the type of the source. Presence and the bound of 8 bits per column are established by the preparation and carried by the group's type; the bound also keeps every equality table at 256 entries or fewer. The dense binds of the family allocate a vector per bind of 1,024 entries or more, as the shared type does for every prover; that is `d·T/16` multiplications of work per core, within the allocation bound of a core, and is the one place where a table of this crate is not bound between two alternating buffers. While a column has fewer than four binds its value on a block of `2^k` cycles is the XOR of `2^k` lookups in tables pre-scaled by the equality weights of the bound challenges, so rounds 1 to 4 cost one lookup per column and cycle and no multiplication; the fourth bind materialises the column at `T/16`.

The summand has `d + 1` linear factors per pair: the weight and the `d` columns. They are paired as `(W·Ra_0), (Ra_1·Ra_2), …`, each pair one `quadratic`, with a last factor left linear when `d` is even. The quadratics are evaluated at the nodes by `mul_x` and multiplied pointwise, the last multiplication at each node fused into the accumulator. There is no linear factor to pull out, so the message is assembled by `coefficients_from_nodes` from the values at 0, infinity and the `d − 1` nodes, with the value at 1 from the claim. For five columns that is 9 multiplications for three quadratics, 12 at the six points, and one for the bind of the weight.

`combined_weight` forms each `Eq` term as a product of two half tables, with the term's coefficient folded into the high one, and accumulates the terms of a cycle unreduced with one reduction per cycle. A `Next` term reads the same half tables at the halves of `j − 1`; it contributes nothing at `j = 0`, which the loop tests before it subtracts. A term whose point lies in `{0, 1}^log_t` adds its coefficient to one entry.

With `ChunkWeight::EqTerms` the core holds no weight table. The product `q` of the `d` columns is formed once per pair and each term weights its values with its own `split_eq(point_i, Some(κ_i))`. For `d ≥ 2` the values are those at 0, at infinity and at the `d − 2` nodes, and each term recovers its own `q(1)` from its own claim. For `d = 1` the product is the column itself, linear in the round variable: it is known from its value at 0 and its leading coefficient, there is no node, `q(1)` is their sum, and the term's claim is not read. For five columns and `m` terms the rounds cost, per cycle, `16.3125 M + 5m A + 14 X + 25 L`, against `16.3125 M + (6 + m) A + 1 R + 18 X + 25 L` with a dense weight. The 14 shifts are six for each of the two quadratics as Performance prices them, which serve nodes 2, 3 and 4, and two for the column left linear (`v·X` and `v·X^2`); the dense form has a third quadratic and no linear factor, 18 shifts. The core holds 24 bytes per cycle less. A `Next` term is not a product over the bits of `j` and has no such form, so a weight with one is `Dense`.

#### Reduction of committed claims

*`g_pass_digits(source, map, weights)`* builds the tables from a `CycleSource`. A `ColumnMap` lists, for ranges of the 256 columns, what holds them: `Word { start, trace_word }` for 64 columns that are the bits of a trace word; `Indicators { start, column }` for the `2^bits − 1` columns `start + k − 1` that are the bits `digit(column, j) = Some(k)`, `k ≥ 1`; and `Flags { start, columns }` for one to eight consecutive columns `start + i` that are the bits `digit(columns[i], j) = Some(0)` of digit columns of zero bits. A word costs eight byte lookups in tables `Λ_p[v] = Σ_{i<8} v[i]·L[8p + i]` of 256 entries, an indicator range one lookup by digit, and a flag range of `n` columns one lookup by its `n` presence bits in a table of `2^n` entries. One parallel pass writes all tables. The map and the supports of the weight vectors are compiled once per call: ranges are grouped by kind and by the set of weight vectors that are non-zero on them, a range no weight touches is dropped, and an entry of a group's table holds the values for all of the group's weights side by side, so that two weights on one digit cost one index and adjacent loads. The loop over the cycles then has no decision that depends on a range. A tile of cycles accumulates each group straight into the output columns its weights name, which the compiled plan fixes before the loop; there is no scratch per group to clear and merge. For the three weight vectors of the experiment that is 37 lookups per cycle in 69 KiB of tables.

*`ReductionCore::new(tables, legs)`.* A leg with point `t` holds a `split_eq(t, None)` and has the round polynomial `σ·(1 + t_i + X)·(A + X·B)`, where `B = Σ eq_rest·(G_lo + G_hi)` over the pairs and `A + t_i·B` is the leg's claim divided by `σ`. The core keeps that quotient per leg: it starts as the leg's claim and becomes `A + r·B` at each challenge `r`, so no round divides. The quotient is what carries a leg through a challenge `r = 1 + t_i`: `σ` becomes zero there and stays zero, the leg's later messages are zero, and its quotient and its table go on being bound, so the table's final value is right and a second leg on the same table is not disturbed. Per pair a leg costs one `fmadd` for `B`, and each table one multiplication for its bind; legs on one table share the bind. The member's message is the sum of the legs' polynomials under their coefficients.

*`column_pass(rows, r)`* forms `e_j = eq(r, j)` with one multiplication per cycle and XORs it into one `ByteBuckets` entry per byte of the row, 32 per cycle; the arrays of a `ScratchPool` are merged by XOR, and the 256 column sums are read out from the 8,192 buckets, which occupy 8,224 elements under the placement of "Lifts and buckets".

#### Fit with `jolt-kernels` and the protocol

A core implements `ProveRounds<F128>`: `prove_round(bind, round, previous_claim)` binds the previous challenge and accumulates the round in one pass, and `finish_rounds` applies the last bind. A core is one `BatchMember` and sees member-local rounds; zero extension and idle rounds are the engine's. `RouterShortCore` is the one member of batch 3a; the five `RouterCycleMember`s are the members of batch 3b and need no scheduler other than `SequentialRounds`.

An implementation of `SumcheckKernel` for a relation of `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md` wraps a core and maps its final values to `output_claims`: `Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)` to the five values of `RouterShort`; the members' source and selector values and the outputs of `claims_pass` to the values of batch 3b; the chunk values to `chunks` of `BytecodeReadCycle` and `RamRaProduct`; the result of `column_pass` to `columns` of `BitsReduction`. These adapters live in `jolt-rv64i-prover/src/optimized/`. `prepare` receives the witness, clones the `Arc`s of the tables a core reads later, and returns the `'static` kernel. Tables that one stage builds for a later one (the prepared groups, the outputs of `fold_pass`, the kept lifts, the scatter plan) are parked in `ProofSession` (`park`, `take`).

No core evaluates the expression of a relation. The input claim and the output expression of a member are evaluated by `Expr::try_evaluate` (`crates/jolt-claims/src/claims.rs`), once per member and proof, by the stage and by the verifier. That evaluator starts each term at its coefficient and multiplies every factor into it, so a term with a unit coefficient pays one multiplication by one, and it calls the resolver of a derived term once per occurrence: the two members of batch 5 pay 25 multiplications for expressions that have 16 products. The cost is per proof and in no figure of Performance. A reference kernel calls the same evaluator per row and node, so the timings of a reference kernel say nothing about a core.

An adapter is accepted, in its own PR, when under the transcript of the reference kernel of its relation: every round's coefficients are equal; every typed output value and every alias the member declares is equal; `validate_derived_tables` passes and each derived term the kernel holds equals the verifier's formula at the final point; and the member's output expression on those values equals its final claim. `run_lockstep` (`crates/jolt-kernels/src/optimized/parity.rs`) compares coefficients and calls `finish_rounds`; it does not call `output_claims`, so it covers the first of the four. `run_lockstep_checked`, which `specs/binary-kernel-primitives.md` adds, covers the four except the equality of an alias with its source in another member, which is a property of the batch. The oracle of the adapters as a set is the proof. With any slots of `Rv64iBackend` filled from `optimized/` and the rest from `reference/`, `Rv64iProof::to_bytes` for the program of the "Proof vector" criterion of `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md` is the encoding whose digest that criterion fixes, and every proof of that spec's end-to-end tests is the byte string the reference kernels produce and verifies. An adapter is built on the witness-plane parameter of `PrepareKernel` (`specs/binary-protocol-family-seams.md`) and on the registries and reference kernels of `specs/rv64i-binary-protocol.md`, which prove its eight batches end to end.

### Alternatives Considered

Counts are per cycle at `log_t = 22`; a saving is given at price point 1 and at price point 2.

- **A fold restricted to the entries `Route` reads.** Fewer bucket XORs, and wrong. The short sum-check hands `Fold_ρ(x|_ρ)` to the cycle member, and that extension reads every entry. With one source variable, `W = [1, 0]` and `Fold = [a, b]`, the round polynomial is `(1 + X)·((1 + X)·a + X·b)`; with `b` dropped it is `(1 + X)^2·a`. Both sum to `a` over `{0, 1}` and they differ by `X·(1 + X)·b`, so the member that evaluates the complete `Source·Select` cannot meet the hand-off.
- **Reuse `optimized/spartan_outer.rs`.** It evaluates rows as integers (`i64`, `i128`), extends them over the centered integer domain by finite differences and accumulates field-by-integer products. None of the three exists in characteristic 2, and here the rows are sum-check variables with bit values. `GruenSplitEqPolynomial` is what carries over.
- **Univariate skip over `F8Domain` for the lane position.** One message of degree 189 needs, per lane group and cycle, the three lanes extended to 63 points of `F8`, 63 products and 63 accumulations against a 128-bit weight: about 156 ns at price point 1 counted the same way, against 158 for rounds 1 to 6, with 187 more field elements in the proof and a different round count.
- **Dense tables from round 1.** 768 elements per cycle, 12 KiB.
- **`accumulate_product_grid`.** It extends factors to the integers `1, …, n − 1` by finite differences; those points are 0 and 1 in characteristic 2.
- **Byte buckets for the four small shapes in the fold pass.** `8·p_shift + 16·p_access + 24·p_key + 16·p_taken` XORs less, 7 ns on the `local` mix, at 72 MiB of buckets per worker for the `Compare` shape alone.
- **Fold writes grouped by selector value.** Sorting the cycles of a block by selector value keeps the buckets of one group in L1. Measured before it was built, `probe` on streams of `2^12` to `2^16` cycles: 0.93 to 1.26 ns per update grouped against 0.83 in cycle order on one thread, and 0.22 to 0.43 against 0.17 to 0.21 on twelve. The cycles of a group write the same sixteen position tables, so a load follows a store to the same bucket, and the update is bound by that dependence and not by the cache. Splitting one 4 KiB table into 2, 4 or 8 copies written in rotation does not remove it: 0.38 to 0.57 ns per update against 0.21 for one copy, in a probe run for this decision and not kept. These figures were taken before the placement of "Lifts and buckets" and have not been repeated under it. Not built.
- **A plan per shape compiled before the fold pass, with the cycle loop reading bank storage and destinations from it.** Built and measured against the loop it replaced: 14 to 15% slower on one thread at `log_t` 20 and 22 with forced inlining, the pass over the rows included. Removed; the cycle and row loops are specialised by the shape's slots at their call site. What the plan computed once is kept where it is not in a loop: the validation of a layout and the offsets of the read-out.
- **Hoists in the cycle and row loops of the fold.** Four were tried on the loops as built: the shapes paired with their layouts once per chunk, the spans of a shape's words cached, the bytecode index of a cycle read once, and the widths of the factor columns lifted out of the cycle loop. Seven trials, the four alone and three combinations, five alternating samples each against the loops as built, on one thread at `log_t` 20 and 22 and on twelve at 22. None improved the minimum by 2% at both sizes; none is kept.
- **One scatter table per worker.** `bytecode_rows()` elements per worker: 192 MiB on 12 workers at `2^20` rows, and every page a visited row touches becomes resident. It is measured by `probe` and not built.
- **Atomic XOR for the scatter.** Correct, since each half of an element is XORed independently, but every worker writes the rows of a hot loop.
- **`(row, weight)` pairs through cursors.** The scatter as first built: every scatter read each bytecode index from the source, advanced a cursor per chunk and range, and stored the row beside the weight, 20 bytes per cycle. All of it but the weight is a function of the source. With the partition kept in the plan a scatter stores 16 bytes per cycle and loads 4; a review prototype ran at about a fifth of the time on a trace whose chunks each fall in one range and at four fifths on a permuted one, on a loaded machine.
- **A bound table per base word through the cycle rounds.** 20 more multiplications per cycle than the kept lifts and `claims_pass`. The opposite trade, lifting five of the six words again in `claims_pass`, costs 40 lookups (16 ns) and frees 80 bytes per cycle through the cycle rounds; it is not taken while memory is not the constraint.
- **One short core per shape, each a batch member.** A batch member is active on one contiguous window of rounds. A shape that skips a slot between two of its own (slot 10, slots 9–11, slots 6–11) is active on two windows.
- **A `RoundScheduler` that owns the cycle pass.** `prove_batch` accepts one, and it removes the lock. The members would then produce no message under `SequentialRounds`, which is what a stage driver passes.
- **Dense chunk columns from round 1.** Five multiplications and 80 bytes per cycle for five columns, in place of 25 lookups.
- **`G` tables of half length.** Round 1 of the reduction needs only the lifts of the XOR of adjacent rows, so the first stored tables can have `T/2` entries, at a second pass over the rows after the first challenge: 36 bytes per cycle in place of 72 for three tables with their buffers. Not taken while memory is not the constraint.
- **A table builder fused with `column_pass`.** `column_pass` needs the challenge point of the rounds that the builder precedes.

Two uses of the structure of the routers are candidates, and a third is rejected until a measurement says otherwise. Each is prover-only. None of the three is built here, and none is built before the cores above are measured at both sizes: each saving below is a count against a model, and the models are conditional on the unit table.

- **The fold of the 16-slot shape keyed by wiring pattern.** That shape's tensor is a sum of wires, each copying a source word to an output word under one of 12 bit patterns. Byte buckets keyed by (output word, pattern), filled with all 64 bits of each wired word, give the shape's six bit rounds on at most 144 pairs of 64-entry tables; the table over (word slot, selector value) that the remaining rounds need comes from one `fmadd` of `e_j` with each trace word's lift at `r_bit`. Per cycle: `8 Bk` per wire of the cycle's selector value and `8 Bk + 1 M + 5 A`, against `88 Bk`. With 4.6 wires per cycle, an estimate, the saving is 22 to 25 ns at point 1 and 25 to 28 at point 2, in at most 4.5 MiB of buckets. It gives the short core a second representation of one shape and a pass over the cycles in its sixth round.
- **One evaluation for the five cycle members.** The members share `eq(r_cycle, ·)` and the batch sends the weighted sum of their polynomials. With each member's batching coefficient folded into its kind selector, the weighted summand nests as `Src_V·Var + P_0·(P_1·(Src_S·SK + Src_C·KK) + Src_M·AK) + Src_B·Br·SB` and costs `26 M + 4 A` per pair against `31 M + 16 A`: 22.4 ns less at point 1 and 12.9 at point 2. It yields one polynomial for the group and none per member, so it needs `prove_batch` to accept a weighted polynomial for a declared set of members and to keep one running claim for the set; `MemberRound` carries no weight today. The same nest as one relation of degree 5 in place of the five members costs the same and changes the transcript; it is not proposed.
- **Rejected until measured: an activity bitmap for the pairs on which a shape's selector is zero.** A pair of round `i` covers `2^i` cycles and contributes nothing when none of them has the shape's kind digit. On the `local` mix with independent cycles the pairs that disappear over the cycle rounds are 77% of the pairs of `Shift`, 26% of `Memory`, 45% of `Compare` and 74% of `Branch`, which is 37.3 ns of product work at point 1 and 19.6 at point 2. The baseline of that figure is the model of Performance, which prices every pair and takes no credit for the skip the cycle core makes in its lazy rounds. About 37.1 of the 37.3 ns are in rounds 1 to 4, where that skip already applies. Over the core as designed a bitmap saves the product work of the dense rounds, about 0.2 ns at point 1, and replaces the support tests of the lazy rounds; it costs one activity bit per cycle and shape (four bitmaps of `T/8` bytes), an OR fold at each bind and a walk over the set bits. The measurement that could reopen it is of the lazy skip and the gathers of the core as designed, on a trace and not on independent cycles.
